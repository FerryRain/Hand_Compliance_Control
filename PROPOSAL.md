# Research proposal: inverse demonstrations for whole-hand tactile exploration

This document is the persistent research-level scope for the repository. Read it
with [`CONTROL_STRATEGIES.md`](CONTROL_STRATEGIES.md) before `PROCESS.md` and
the process document of the active subproject.

## Central claim

The main contribution is not an online nonlinear optimizer that jointly solves
the wrist and four fingertips. The proposed system is:

> scalable inverse demonstration generation + wrist-conditioned finger
> Diffusion Policy (DP) + wrist-only ER-GPIS active exploration.

It moves expensive high-dimensional coordination from the online loop into
parallelizable offline data generation and learning. At deployment time,
ER-GPIS searches only a low-dimensional wrist action space. The learned finger
policy uses wrist motion and tactile/contact history to coordinate the fingers.

The explicit whole-hand optimization implemented in `full_hand_mcc/` is
**Baseline 2**, not the main method. Its current MCC implementation is the
low-level controller used by that baseline.

## Research question

Can scalable inverse demonstrations teach a policy to coordinate multi-finger
contact implicitly, so that active whole-hand tactile exploration requires only
wrist-level planning online while retaining contact and running in real time?

The decomposition is:

- wrist action: planned by ER-GPIS;
- finger action: generated by a wrist-conditioned finger DP;
- wrist execution: Minimalist Compliance Control (MCC);
- safety: a lightweight feasibility and recovery layer.

There is no whole-hand nonlinear optimizer in the high-frequency main-method
control loop.

The two low-level variants are defined precisely in
[`CONTROL_STRATEGIES.md`](CONTROL_STRATEGIES.md):

- main method: Wrist MCC + fingertip-force-conditioned Finger DP, with no
  additional Finger MCC;
- Baseline 2: Wrist MCC + four normal-direction, real-fingertip-force-based
  Cartesian admittance controllers.

MCC means external-force-driven virtual mass–damping–stiffness reference
integration. Motor current is only one possible wrench-estimation source and
is not part of the definition.

## Contributions

### 1. Scalable inverse demonstration generation

Generate easy forward trajectories with scripted motion, IK, or simple
controllers; invert their action sequence; replay the inverse candidate in the
physics simulator; re-record all state, tactile, contact, force, and slip
signals; then automatically filter failures.

This is not a claim that contact dynamics are analytically reversible. The
training trajectory is the result of physics replay of an inverted candidate,
not a reversed sensor log.

### 2. Wrist-conditioned implicit multi-finger compliance

Train a finger DP whose observations include finger positions and velocities,
tactile/contact features, contact forces, contact validity, and previous finger
action. It is additionally conditioned on a future wrist-motion chunk
(translation, rotation, linear velocity, and angular velocity).

The policy outputs finger position increments with action chunking and
receding-horizon execution. It does not receive explicit fingertip targets.

### 3. Wrist-level active exploration

ER-GPIS proposes wrist-only actions. A fast arm-feasibility filter and a learned
contact-retention predictor score whether the finger DP is likely to execute
each action safely. The objective combines information gain, predicted contact
success, motion cost, joint-margin risk, and collision risk.

## First-version research boundary

- The object is fixed.
- Four physical fingertip pads are the main contacts.
- Palm contact is not required.
- The wrist may translate and rotate over a limited workspace.
- ER-GPIS is updated incrementally from contact observations.
- DP controls only the fingers; the arm/wrist uses MCC.
- Object motion in the hand is outside the first version.

## Phase 1: inverse-data pipeline

Implement and validate:

1. forward data generation;
2. action inversion;
3. physics replay;
4. automatic filtering;
5. success/failure dataset export.

Each time step must retain at least:

- finger joint position and velocity;
- finger action label;
- wrist pose and velocity;
- tactile observation/features;
- per-finger contact force;
- per-finger contact validity.

For inversion, joint and wrist translation increments are time-reversed with
opposite sign. Wrist rotations are inverted as rotation matrices or
quaternions; Euler angles must not simply be negated. During replay, all
observations and labels are sampled again from the simulator.

The initial domain randomization should cover object geometry and scale, pose,
friction, contact stiffness, initial finger configuration, initial wrist pose,
wrist direction/rotation, and wrist speed. Start with a sphere and one wrist
translation direction, then expand to cylinders, ellipsoids, boxes,
superquadrics, and complex meshes.

Filtering must measure contact-retention rate, simultaneous contact count,
contact force range, joint margin, slip, self-collision, unintended contact,
penetration, and simulation instability. Successful replay trains DP; both
successful and failed replay train the contact-retention predictor.

## Phase 2: finger DP without ER-GPIS

First isolate the learned contact controller from active exploration:

- Test A: fixed wrist translations along multiple axes.
- Test B: fixed wrist rotations about three axes.
- Test C: combined curved, varying-speed, stop-and-restart trajectories.

Report contact retention, force stability, joint-limit violations, contact
recovery, and inference latency. The suggested hierarchy is 200–500 Hz joint
servo, 50–200 Hz tactile estimation, 10–30 Hz finger DP, and 1–5 Hz wrist
planning/model update.

## Phase 3: contact-retention predictor

Train a lightweight predictor from the replay success and failure data. Given
the current observation, a candidate wrist twist, and its duration, it should
first predict an overall success probability. Later versions may predict
per-finger contact probability, maximum contact force, and minimum joint margin.

## Phase 4: wrist-only ER-GPIS

Bootstrap an initial surface from contact and normal-offset dummy points.
Generate a small batch of discrete local wrist translations, rotations,
translation/rotation combinations, stop actions, and slight retreats. Filter
arm IK, joint, workspace, speed, and collision violations. Rank the remainder
by information gain, DP success probability, motion cost, joint risk, and
collision risk.

The first version may predict candidate fingertip locations from rigid wrist
motion projected onto the local GPIS tangent plane. A learned contact-outcome
model can replace this approximation later.

## Phase 5: deployment and recovery

The wrist executes the selected action with task tracking plus MCC compliance.
The finger DP executes a safe projection of its predicted position increments.
The safety projection enforces joint position/velocity, maximum contact force,
self-collision, and single-step action bounds.

The main method must not add a full Finger MCC correction on top of DP output.
Real fingertip force is a DP observation and a safety/recovery signal. Stacking
another force controller would change the learned action semantics and create
training/deployment distribution shift.

The runtime state machine must include:

- Normal Exploration: enough contacts and safe forces.
- Contact Recovery: pause/retreat the wrist and recover finger contact.
- Force Release: retreat along the estimated normal and reduce finger closure.
- Workspace Reset: reposition the wrist when fingers approach joint limits.

Brief contact loss is allowed. Recovery must be bounded and must restore the
required contact support before active exploration resumes.

## Baseline 2: explicit whole-hand optimization

`full_hand_mcc/` implements the low-level controller for this baseline. The
baseline receives wrist and fingertip targets and explicitly solves wrist pose,
finger joints, and slack under fingertip tracking, reachability,
manipulability, joint-limit, collision, and smoothness terms.

Its four Finger MCC loops directly consume measured fingertip forces. Each loop
projects force onto the local surface normal, integrates a scalar
mass–damping–stiffness admittance offset, corrects only the normal component of
the planned Cartesian fingertip target, and sends the result through finger
IK/Jacobian control. It does not reconstruct fingertip force from hand motor
current. Finger loops run faster than the lower-gain wrist loop to reduce
closed-loop conflict.

Two variants are required:

- **Baseline 2A — Optimization Oracle:** allow convergence without a real-time
  requirement and record solve time.
- **Baseline 2B — Time Capped:** impose 50 ms, 100 ms, and 200 ms budgets and
  use the best current or previous solution on timeout.

It exists to expose the contact-performance versus compute-time tradeoff. The
main method should approach the slow oracle's contact/exploration performance
with much lower online latency.

## Experiments

Data-generation ablations compare raw forward data, inverted actions without
replay, replay without filtering, and the full inversion + replay + filtering
pipeline.

Policy ablations isolate wrist conditioning, tactile input, and temporal
history.

Active-exploration comparisons include fixed fingers, rule-based force closing,
explicit whole-hand optimization, inverse-trained DP with uncertainty-only
wrist planning, inverse-trained DP with information + success scoring, and the
slow optimization oracle.

Report:

- trajectories per GPU-hour and valid inverse-replay rate;
- data collection cost and state/object coverage;
- multi-finger contact retention, average contact count, force statistics,
  slip, recovery, joint violations, and inference latency;
- Chamfer distance, reconstruction completeness, uncertainty reduction,
  information gain per second/distance, contact coverage, exploration time,
  and wrist path length;
- mean, P95, and maximum online latency, deadline misses, and achieved rate.

## Development order

1. Run inverse generation end-to-end on one sphere and one wrist translation.
2. Train a small finger DP and compare it with fixed/rule-based fingers.
3. Add directions, wrist rotations, curvature, friction, and unseen objects.
4. Train the contact-retention predictor.
5. Add discrete wrist-level ER-GPIS candidate selection.
6. Complete the slow and time-capped explicit optimization baselines.
7. Perform sim-to-real transfer.

The current `full_hand_mcc/` work belongs to step 6 and may also supply an
oracle/teacher trajectory source for step 1. It must not silently expand into
the online architecture of the proposed main method.
